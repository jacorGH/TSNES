Edit tiles and sprites

Tap Pick, then tap a character or tile, or drag across an area to grab a whole object. Edits show everywhere those tiles appear, in the game’s own colors.

Sprite sheets and animations

Pick a character (or an animated tile area) and tap “Record frames”, then play: every pose and direction is captured. The sheet opens as one picture to edit, or to export as a PNG and redraw in your art app.

AI makeover: free, works with any AI

1. Say what you want, or tap an idea. Choose “Redraw” with a recorded sprite sheet to get new art for a character.

2. Copy the prompt the game writes and paste it into any AI chat, like ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude, free versions included. It describes the screen in words, so a picture is optional; attaching the screenshot can help.

3. Copy the AI’s whole answer, then come back and tap “Paste and apply”.

Ask Claude directly (your own API key)

Same room

Room codes use a free public service to introduce the phones; direct invites skip it entirely (you swap two short codes instead). Either way the game then goes straight between phones, and the host passes messages between friends so everyone hears everyone. If a friend’s network blocks direct connections, set up your own relay in Settings. The delay suits co-op and turn-based games best. Multitap games (3–4 players) aren’t supported yet.

Drifting petals, falling leaves, snow, rain or fireflies over the game. Fireflies come out at night when lighting is on.

Screen

3D

Works for SNES and Game Boy games. Characters stand up like paper cutouts on a 3D stage, with soft shadows and a tilt-shift blur at the edges. Shadows follow the time of day when Lighting is on. From Tools, Pick, mark tiles to stand up, glow at night (lanterns, windows) or shimmer (water). Move with phone gently follows how you hold it and settles back to center.

Your relay

A relay carries the game when a friend’s network blocks direct connections (some cellular, school and work networks). Home Wi-Fi usually doesn’t need one.

Setup guide

Cloudflare (recommended). Free for the first 1,000 GB of relayed data each month, which is hundreds of hours of play. Everything works from your phone:

1. In the Cloudflare dashboard, open Realtime, then TURN, and create a TURN key. Keep its Key ID and API token.
2. Open Workers & Pages, create a Worker from the Hello World template, deploy it, then edit its code: replace it with relay/cloudflare-worker.js from your repo and deploy again.
3. In the Worker’s Settings, Variables and secrets, add TURN_KEY_ID, TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN (as a secret) and ACCESS_CODE (a secret you make up).
4. Here, pick Cloudflare Worker, paste the Worker’s address and your access code, and tap Save and test.
The Worker only hands out logins that expire within a day, and only to someone with your access code.

Your own server. On a free Ubuntu cloud server (Oracle’s Always Free tier works), run sudo bash setup-coturn.sh https://YOU.github.io/YOUR-REPO/ from the repo’s relay folder. It installs the relay and prints a link; open it on your phone to save the relay here. Also open UDP and TCP 3478 and UDP 49160–49260 in the cloud provider’s firewall.

Sharing. With sharing on, your invites carry the relay logins so a friend on a strict network can still connect. Anyone holding that invite link could use your relay until the logins expire (Cloudflare) or until you change the password (own server).
